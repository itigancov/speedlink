A slow walk along the river path

By Mira Alder · 6 min read

The morning started with fog over the water, the kind that makes the far bank a rumour. We left the car by the old mill and followed the towpath north, where the hawthorn was already heavy with berries.

By nine the fog had thinned to a few threads between the willows. A heron stood in the shallows as if it had been placed there, and did not move for the whole of our slow approach.

Further on, the path climbs away from the river and the sounds change: the rush of water gives way to wind in the beech leaves, and somewhere below a dog, a long way off, announcing us to no one.

We stopped at the stile for tea. The notes from this stretch are short, because there was little to add to what the place already said.
